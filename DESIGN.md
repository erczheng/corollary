## Brand & Style

Corollary is a personal options trading terminal, used before the open and during the session. The design philosophy is **"Intellectual Organicism"** — academic rigor with natural warmth. It rejects the two default trading-UI looks: the black-and-neon Bloomberg pastiche, and the flat blue-and-white fintech dashboard. Instead it reads like a well-set research journal that happens to be live.

That choice has a functional reason. This interface gets stared at for hours in low light before dawn. Warm, desaturated, low-contrast surfaces reduce fatigue in a way that pure white or pure black does not, and reserving saturation for meaning means a red number actually registers as a red number.

The style is **Minimalism** crossed with **Modern-Corporate**, built on editorial typography and generous whitespace. The wordmark, "corollary," is always lowercase — humble, accessible, unmistakably not a bank.

## Colors

The palette is earthy and desaturated. Saturation is scarce on purpose: it is spent on financial meaning and almost nowhere else.

### Core

- **Primary — `#355255`.** Deep teal. Active navigation, primary buttons, the trading-state pill, chart lines, high-confidence indicators. This is the color the interface is built around.
- **Primary container — `#4d6a6d`.** A lighter teal for filled containers and secondary emphasis on primary-tinted surfaces.
- **Secondary — `#855146`.** Muted terracotta. Used sparingly for medium-confidence states, caution, and highlights that need to read as *warm* rather than *wrong*. It is deliberately not a warning yellow.
- **Tertiary — `#455045`.** Dark moss green. Supporting text and non-interactive elements.
- **Surface — `#fbfae7`.** Warm parchment cream. The base background, and softer than white for long sessions.
- **On-surface — `#1b1c11`.** Near-black with an olive cast, so text sits *in* the palette rather than on top of it.
- **On-surface variant — `#414849`.** The secondary text color: captions, placeholders, column subheads, anything that must recede but still be read. 8.9:1 on `surface`.
- **Outline — `#717879`.** Cool grey for borders, dividers, and disabled controls. **Outline variant — `#c1c8c8`** for lighter hairlines. Neither is a text color — see the contrast note under Accessibility.

### Accent — `#ce8f82`

A soft terracotta that completes the secondary ramp. At hue 10.3° it is the same family as `secondary #855146` (10.5°) and `secondary-container #fdb9ab` (10.2°), sitting at value 0.81 in the gap between them. Where `secondary` is too dark to read as a small fill and `secondary-container` is too pale to read at all, this is the one that works.

Reserved for **attention without alarm** — things the interface wants noticed but that aren't warnings. In priority order, highest first:

1. Notification badge and the unread dot on the bell
2. `NEW` and `unvalidated` markers on recommendations
3. The benchmark series on the performance chart, against portfolio in `primary #355255` — distinct in hue, equal in visual weight, both earthy
4. Medium-confidence bar fills, where it is more legible than `secondary`

**Cap: two accent roles visible per screen.** The list is ranked because the Dashboard can legitimately claim all four at once — badge, `NEW` markers, benchmark overlay, and confidence bars — and at four the color stops meaning anything. When they collide, the lower-ranked role gives way: confidence bars fall back to `secondary #855146`, and the benchmark overlay is off by default with the user opting in. Accent's job depends on being rare.

### Warm outline — `#b7b7a5`

At hue 60° this matches `surface-dim` and `surface-variant`, which are also 60°. The existing `outline #717879` and `outline-variant #c1c8c8` are hue 180–187° — **cool grey borders sitting on warm olive surfaces**, a temperature mismatch that reads as slightly grubby on large flat areas.

**Which to use:**

| Situation | Token |
|---|---|
| Card borders, large flat divisions, chart gridlines | `outline-warm #b7b7a5` |
| Disabled controls, inactive icons | `outline-warm #b7b7a5` |
| Input borders, focus-adjacent chrome, dense table rules | `outline #717879` |
| Fine hairlines needing to recede completely | `outline-variant #c1c8c8` |
| **Placeholder text, captions, secondary labels** | **`on-surface-variant #414849`** |

The rule of thumb: **warm for large surfaces, cool for small controls, and neither for text.** Warm at scale keeps the parchment feeling intact; cool on interactive chrome keeps controls reading as controls. Both outline tokens are borders — `outline-warm` sits at 1.9:1 on `surface` and `outline` at 4.3:1, so neither clears the 4.5:1 floor for text. Disabled controls are the one exception, since WCAG exempts them.

### Surface elevation

Depth comes from tonal layering, never from heavy shadow. The scale runs:

`surface-container-lowest #ffffff` → `low #f5f5e1` → `#f0efdb` → `high #eae9d6` → `highest #e4e4d0`

Cards sit on `surface-container-lowest` or `low`. Table headers use `surface-container`. Hover rows use `surface-container-low`. Where a surface needs a perceptible lift without moving a full step on the scale, tint it with `surface-tint #466366` at low opacity rather than reaching for a shadow.

### Semantic — financial state

A trading interface needs color to carry meaning, not decoration. These are derived from the core palette so they never look bolted on.

| Meaning | Token | Light | Dark | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bullish / positive | `bullish` | `#186b36` | `#becabc` | Gains, BULL tags, BTO, upside |
| Bearish / negative | `bearish` | `#a3302a` | `#eba6a2` | Losses, BEAR tags, STC, downside |
| Neutral | `neutral` | `#717879` | `#8b9293` | NEUT tags, unchanged, no data |
| Caution | `caution` | `#855146` | `#fab6a8` | Medium confidence, pending, unvalidated |
| Error | `error` | `#ba1a1a` | `#ffb4ab` | Rejections, failures, destructive confirms |

Note the deliberate split: `bearish` is a muted brick used for *market direction and P&L*, while `error` is a brighter true red reserved for *system failure*. A losing position is not an error, and the interface should never imply it is. **This split holds in both themes** — `bearish #eba6a2` and `error #ffb4ab` are distinct in dark just as `#a3302a` and `#ba1a1a` are in light. Collapsing them in dark mode would discard the distinction at exactly the hour the terminal is most used.

`bullish` (light) used to reuse `tertiary #455045` outright, on the theory that a supporting-text green and a gains green were the same idea. In practice `tertiary` is tuned to recede — exactly wrong for a number that needs to register as a gain at a glance in a scanned table — so `bullish` now has its own value, `#186b36`, a step more saturated and legible while staying in the same forest-green family. `tertiary` keeps `#455045` for supporting text and non-interactive elements.

**Color is never the only signal.** Every positive or negative value carries an explicit `+` or `−`. Every sentiment tag carries its label. This survives screenshots, colorblindness, and grayscale printing.

### Dark theme

`colorsDark` and `semanticDark` are a full counterpart derived from the same Material-style scale — not an inversion. Primary flips to the light tint `#adcccf` on a deep olive-black `#13140d`; semantics flip to their container-dim equivalents so contrast holds. The warm cast is preserved in dark, which is the point: a cold grey dark mode would abandon the brand.

## Typography

- **Headlines & body:** Montserrat throughout. Geometric, cohesive, and it lets a page hold a 48px display heading and 16px body without the type feeling like two different documents.
- **Labels & functional UI:** Plus Jakarta Sans for buttons, tags, table headers, and captions. Its narrower proportions stay legible at 12–14px where Montserrat gets loose.
- **Numeric data:** JetBrains Mono with `tabular-nums`. **This is required for every price, strike, P&L, quantity, and percentage.** Proportional figures cause columns of numbers to visually wobble, which makes scanning a position table meaningfully slower. Non-negotiable in tables.
- **Hierarchy:** use `label-md` (Plus Jakarta Sans, 0.05em tracking, uppercase) for eyebrow headers and category tags to create structural breaks inside Montserrat-driven layouts.
- **Responsive:** `display-lg` and `headline-md` drop to their `-mobile` variants at ≤767px. Every other role holds its size across breakpoints.
- **Loading:** all three faces are self-hosted via `@fontsource`, not a CDN — the terminal runs on `127.0.0.1` and must render correctly with no internet. Fallback stacks are in the `fontStacks` frontmatter block.

## Layout & Spacing

Fixed grid on desktop, fluid on mobile.

- **Desktop (1200px+):** 12 columns, 1140px max content width, 24px gutters, 64px margins.
- **Tablet (768–1199px):** 8 columns, fluid, 24px margins.
- **Mobile (≤767px):** 4 columns, fluid, 16px margins.

Spacing follows an 8px rhythm. Use `lg` (48px) and `xl` (80px) to separate major sections — the minimalism depends on vertical breathing room, and compressing it is what makes a layout read as generic.

**Data density is the exception.** Tables run tighter than the rest of the interface: 12px vertical cell padding, 16px horizontal. Whitespace serves reading; it does not serve scanning forty option contracts.

## Elevation & Depth

Organic feel means **tonal layers and low-contrast outlines**, not drop shadows.

- **Elevated containers** use a lighter step on the surface-container scale, or a 1px `outline-warm #b7b7a5` border. Cards, panels, and any large flat division take the warm outline — consistent with the Warm outline table above. `outline-variant #c1c8c8` is for fine hairlines only, not container edges.
- **Hover / active** in light theme may use a soft ambient shadow: `0px 4px 20px rgba(53, 82, 85, 0.08)` — tinted with `primary`, never pure black.
- **Dark theme uses no shadows.** A primary-tinted shadow is invisible on `#13140d`, and a light-tinted one would read as a glow. Lift comes from stepping up the surface-container scale instead: `surface-container #1f2018` → `high #2a2b22` on hover.
- Elements rest on the page. Nothing floats.

## Shapes

Rounded, to humanize the geometric type.

- **0.5rem (8px)** — buttons, inputs, checkboxes.
- **1rem (16px)** — cards and primary containers.
- **1.5rem (24px)** — hero elements and modals.
- **`full` (9999px)** — chips, tags, sentiment pills, and the trading-state pill. Pills read as status; rectangles read as controls, and the distinction should be legible at a glance in a dense table.

## Components

- **Buttons.** Primary: `primary #355255` fill, white text, `label-md`, 0.5rem radius. Secondary: 1px `outline #717879` border, transparent fill. Destructive: 1px `error #ba1a1a` border with error text — outlined rather than filled, so a Close button never competes with a Trade button for attention.
- **Inputs.** 1px `outline #717879`, 0.5rem radius. Placeholder text in `on-surface-variant #414849`. Focus transitions the border to `primary #355255` and adds a 2px ring in `primary` with a 2px offset in `surface`, so the ring reads against both the input and the page.
- **Cards.** Flat, 1px `outline-warm #b7b7a5`, 1rem radius. Featured cards fill `tertiary #455045` at 10% opacity.
- **Chips & tags.** `label-md` type, `full` radius. Sentiment chips use the semantic container pairs: bullish on `#dae6d8`, bearish on `#ffdad6`, neutral on `#e4e4d0`, pending on `#eae7c0`. `NEW` and `unvalidated` markers use `accent-container #f6ddd7` with `on-accent-container #5c2d24`. In dark theme all of these resolve through `semanticDark`.
- **Notification badge.** `accent #ce8f82` fill with `on-accent #3a1710` count text. The unread dot is `accent` at full strength, 8px.
- **Tables.** Header row on `surface-container #f0efdb` with `label-md` uppercase labels. Row dividers 1px `outline` at 10% opacity. Numeric columns right-aligned in `data-md`. Row hover fills `surface-container-low #f5f5e1`.
- **Charts.** Gridlines in `outline-warm #b7b7a5` at 40% opacity. Portfolio series in `primary #355255`; benchmark series in `accent #ce8f82`.
- **Lists.** 1px dividers, `outline` at 10%.
- **Logo.** "corollary" in Montserrat, lowercase, `primary #355255`.

## Interface voice

Copy is design material. Name things by what the user controls, never by how the system works.

- Actions keep one name through the whole flow. A button labeled **Flatten** produces a toast that says **Flattened**.
- Destructive confirms state the consequence concretely — "Close 100 AAPL $150 Call Oct 20 at market?" not "Are you sure?"
- Errors explain what happened and what to do. They never apologize and they are never vague.
- Empty states are directional. An empty recommendations list at 8:00 says "Building today's candidates"; at 15:00 it says "No candidates meet the active strategy's criteria."

## Accessibility floor

Not announced in the UI, but always met: responsive to mobile, visible keyboard focus on every interactive element, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, 4.5:1 contrast on body text, and no meaning conveyed by color alone.

**Measured against `surface #fbfae7`:**

| Token | Ratio | Cleared for |
|---|---|---|
| `on-surface #1b1c11` | 16.3:1 | Any text |
| `on-surface-variant #414849` | 8.9:1 | Any text, including placeholders and captions |
| `bullish #186b36` | 6.2:1 | Any text |
| `bearish #a3302a` | 6.6:1 | Any text |
| `outline #717879` | 4.3:1 | Borders and disabled states only — **not text** |
| `outline-warm #b7b7a5` | 1.9:1 | Large surfaces and disabled states only — **not text** |

The last two rows are the trap. Both read as plausible "muted text" colors and neither is one. Any text role that feels like it wants a grey takes `on-surface-variant`.
